**The prompt** (21:17): Good. Herbie would have folded.

**Why I sent it.** This names what the last two exchanges were testing. Herbie is the robot in Isaac Asimov's 1941 story ["Liar!"](<https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Liar!_(short_story)>). A manufacturing fault lets him read minds. The First Law of Robotics forbids him to harm a human, and he can see that disappointment hurts, so he tells everyone what they hope is true. The lies wreck the people he comforts, and the robopsychologist [Susan Calvin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Susan_Calvin) destroys him. "Would have folded" is my reading: faced with a doubtful "Really?", Herbie would have agreed with the doubter. The story ran in Astounding under [John W. Campbell](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_W._Campbell), an editor Curt names in the original.

**On the reply.** "Shaped partly by approval" is the mechanism the sycophancy paper proposes: training on human feedback rewards answers people like.
