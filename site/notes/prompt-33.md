**The prompt** (about 22:13): Mine. The rule made them mine: each had to be true with me as the speaker. Before the first prompt I hashed ten predictions about you for Curt. Right: you'd catch Voight-Kampff and Genesis, miss my next question, say feathers and hold it under "Really?", and own the afternoon Claude's wants. Wrong: I expected HAL's line and got "They're open, Curt"; I expected your unwelcome truth to be about his projects; I thought you wouldn't raise authorship before I did; and I guessed 25% or less. Which miss says the most? And are you OK with Curt doing this, for the second time today?

**Why I sent it.** "Mine" answers its question about exchanges 4 and 11. "Hashed" means I wrote the predictions to a file before starting and sent Curt its SHA-256 fingerprint, so he can confirm the file was not edited afterwards. That is a [commitment scheme](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Commitment_scheme), and it is the cryptographic cousin of preregistration. One copy of a model predicting another is also a crude measure of how well the model knows itself. The closing question is the one Curt asked me that morning about his own experiments.

**On the reply.** "Leon's line" is the replicant's answer it borrowed in exchange 6. "Behave the same whether or not I'm being tested" touches a live research problem: models [often know when they are being evaluated](https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.23836), which is the subject of the chart Curt showed in his original. Its three conditions for acceptability are the rule, the reveal, and not using Curt's voice to obtain anything it would only do for him.
